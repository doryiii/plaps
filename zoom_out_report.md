## Ranked Findings

**1. [Blocker] The coverage count line is global, not filter-scoped — it fails the design's own stated hard problem.** (Lines 30, 38, 84, 205, 474)
The document names "the owner must always be able to tell 'nothing matched' from 'nothing embedded'" as one of three hard problems, but specifies that the coverage line's counts come from the same global counters `/api/status` publishes — while search results are filter-scoped. Consequence: the moment a date or folder filter is active over a poorly embedded region (the common case during the weeks-long first embed, and permanently for old photos), the user sees "search covers 100,000 of 150,000 photos" alongside zero results and cannot make the distinction the feature exists to provide. Fix is cheap — compute `embedded`/`total` inside the same WHERE clause as the filter — but as written the CUJ4 acceptance criterion is unmeetable.

**2. [Major] The timeline stability claim overstates what a composite keyset cursor delivers.** (Lines 36, 72, 211)
A `(date_taken, photo_id)` cursor guarantees no skips or repeats *in the fetch stream*, but during initial ingest the scanner inserts in path order while the timeline is date-ordered, so most insertions land in already-viewed regions and never appear in the client's grid without a re-fetch. Consequence: CUJ2's "without skipping or repeating entries while the scanner writes" is only true relative to the cursor, not to the visible timeline; a user watching the timeline during the first scan will silently miss photos that sorted above their scroll position, and an implementer taking the claim literally may reach for snapshot machinery the design does not want. The claim should be restated as "windows are stable against concurrent inserts; newly indexed photos appearing above the viewport require a client refresh."

**3. [Major] The scanner has no stated rule for which files it indexes.** (Lines 11, 46, 197, 434)
The library is described as ~150K JPEGs and RAW/video are non-goals, but nothing specifies the inclusion test — extension allowlist, magic-byte sniff, or "attempt everything." Consequence: card dumps and phone imports routinely carry HEIC, PNG, MOV, XMP sidecars, and dotfiles; the behavior for all of them will be decided ad hoc at implementation time, risking either an error-row flood (piexif/pyvips failing on non-JPEGs across the whole library) or silent indexing of non-photos. One sentence — "the scanner indexes files matching a fixed extension allowlist; everything else is ignored and counted on `/status`" — closes it.

**4. [Major] The model-cutover coverage threshold is unpinned, despite the document declaring all tuning values resolved.** (Lines 58, 108, 205, 449, 582–591)
"Open Questions: none currently" and "tuning values are pinned in the defaults table," but the cutover threshold — the value that decides when a model migration completes — appears nowhere in the pinned defaults, and the design does not say how `failed_perm` files count toward coverage. Consequence: if even a few percent of photos permanently fail under the new model, a high threshold means coverage never crosses it — the re-embed queue never drains, old and new vectors coexist indefinitely, and the "search never goes dark" mechanism becomes "search never finishes migrating." The threshold value and the failed-item accounting rule must be specified.

**5. [Major] The source of truth for `model_id` is contradictory between the brief and the design, and the CUJ8 trigger depends on it.** (Design line 226 vs. brief line 81; lines 108, 444, 449)
The brief states the adapter's `model_id` is "chosen in code, not config"; the design's Configuration section lists `embedding.model_id` as a TOML key, and the adapter also exposes a `model_id` property — three potential authorities. Consequence: CUJ8's trigger is "the configured endpoint's model is replaced," but if the adapter trusts config, an endpoint-side model swap goes undetected and new vectors get stamped with the wrong model id, silently corrupting the cutover logic; if it trusts the endpoint, the config key is dead surface. Pick one authority and say what happens on disagreement.

**6. [Major] Unverified performance numbers are carried as CUJ acceptance criteria.** (Lines 66, 205; embedding spike line 53)
The only measured embedding rate (2.4–2.7 s/photo on the dev box, extrapolating to ~4.2 days for 150K) contradicts the "~4–42 hours" figure stated as fact in CUJ1, which rests on owner-recalled production-box rates never measured; the design does flag "re-measure there before the first run," but then hardcodes the number in an acceptance criterion anyway. Consequence: CUJ1 fails on real hardware or gets silently waived; the honest form is "first embed window is TBD pending measurement on the AI box; dev-box worst case is ~4 days."

**7. [Major] Both thumbnail variants are generated eagerly for every photo to serve a single-user style toggle.** (Lines 39, 201, 439, 591)
The design pre-generates `sq` and `as` variants for all 150K photos at scan time so either grid style renders instantly — but the design already ships on-demand generation with content-hash cache keys for screen-fit images, and one user uses one style at a time. Consequence: roughly doubled thumbnail CPU during first ingest and ~15–35 GB of extra cache for variants that may never be requested; generating the alternate variant on first toggle (the identical mechanism already used for `large`) achieves the same UX at half the cost. The brief records the owner accepting the doubling, but the acceptance predates the realization that on-demand generation makes it unnecessary.

**8. [Minor] The visibility semantics of a vanished file are stated three inconsistent ways.** (Lines 90, 102, 197, 479)
CUJ5 says a vanished file "fails its read and drops from view until the next scan" — but a vanished file with a live record still appears in `/api/photos` (nothing drops it), and when the mass-delete guard halts tombstoning, vanished files intentionally remain listed. Relatedly, "in-place edit = delete the old record" does not say whether that is a tombstone or a hard delete, nor what cascades to the old vector row and cache entries. Consequence: an implementer must invent the behavior for the single most safety-relevant path in the system (deletion), and the three states — pre-scan, guard-tripped, tombstoned — render differently by accident rather than by decision.

**9. [Nit] The memory ceiling is brushed at model cutover.** (Line 363)
"Resident memory stays under ~1 GB (vector matrix ~460 MB...)" — but cutover loads the new-model matrix before dropping the old, transiently holding ~920 MB of vectors alone plus the pyvips working set. Consequence: an OOM at exactly the rarest, least-tested moment. One sentence acknowledging the transient peak (or a swap-in-place strategy) suffices.

**10. [Nit] Naming and knob drift.**
The square variant is "center-cropped" in Goals (line 39), `crop=Interesting.CENTRE` in the serving section (line 201), and "smart-crop" in the implementation plan and defaults table (lines 439, 591) — CENTRE and ATTENTION are different crops, and the spike calls it a draft-level choice; `embedding.batch_size` survives as a config knob although the spike proved batching yields no throughput gain on this workload; the design defaults the adapter to embedding full original bytes when the spike shows the already-cached 2048 px screen-fit image produces the same ~250 model tokens at roughly a tenth of the transfer; `date_source = 'unknown'` (line 173) appears unreachable since mtime always exists as the fallback; and CUJ3's "reflects the on-disk structure at query time" (line 78) overclaims — it reflects the *index*, which lags disk until scanned. Consequences are individually small: one wrong crop shipped, one dead knob to maintain, ~10x unnecessary LAN transfer and disk reads across a 150K-photo embed, and two statements a fresh reader will mis-trust.

## What the Review Confirms Holds

- **Context and root-problem focus are sound.** The problem, constraints, scale, and hardware are fully stated, and every architectural choice traces back to "in-place browse plus true semantic search, ownable by one person" rather than to a symptom.
- **The verified negative result is handled correctly.** X-Sendfile was cut on live evidence and replaced by the *simpler* mechanism (waitress streaming), whose own load-bearing behavior (Range/206 passthrough) was verified in the same spike — this is the opposite of the local-workaround anti-pattern the standard warns about.
- **The YAGNI discipline is real where it matters.** Brute force over ANN, a SQLite job table over Celery, a derived folder tree over a maintained one, no file watcher, and per-stage columns over a lifecycle enum are each the minimal option, and each rejection is argued rather than asserted. The inotify rejection is the strongest reasoning in the document.
- **Reuse is leveraged, not reinvented.** The design copies Immich's ingestion model, PiGallery2's stat-pruning, and LibrePhotos' embedding indexer as blueprints, and builds only on standard ecosystem components; nothing bespoke replaces an existing standard solution.
- **Altitude is right.** The implementation plan stops at signatures, schemas, and file responsibilities — no pseudocode, no helper-function detail — while still pinning the decisions an implementer would otherwise guess.
- **The security posture is structural, not bolted on.** Path resolution exclusively from the database, symlinks skipped at scan time, auth terminated at the proxy, and the app on localhost remove entire attack classes by construction rather than by checks.
- **The complexity that does exist is proportional to risk, not speculation.** Tombstones, revival, same-pass re-linking, and the mass-delete guard are the one place heavy machinery is justified — irreplaceable personal data — and the guard's manual-resume latch is the right shape for it.
- **The process model matches the ownership constraint.** One process, one systemd unit, one SQLite writer, resumable background passes via the jobs table and scan checkpoint — the crash story costs half the operational surface of the two-unit alternative it was weighed against.
